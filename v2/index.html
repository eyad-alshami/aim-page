AIM: Amending Inherent Interpretability via Self-Supervised Masking

Eyad Alshami1,2, Shashank Agnihotri3, Bernt Schiele1 and Margret Keuper1,3

1Max Planck Institute for Informatics, Saarland Informatics Campus   2RTG Neuroexplicit Models   3University of Mannheim

ICCV 2025 · 🌟 Highlighted Paper

AIM focuses on the object of interest using nothing but the image label — outperforming baselines in attribution localization, even on challenging datasets like Waterbirds and Hard ImageNet.

Deep neural networks often rely on both genuine and spurious features. AIM is a simple yet effective method that promotes the use of genuine features — without requiring any additional annotations. It uses features from multiple encoding stages to guide a self-supervised, sample-specific feature-masking process, enabling the training of well-performing, inherently interpretable models that faithfully summarize their decision process. Across general-purpose (ImageNet-100, Hard ImageNet, ImageWoof) and fine-grained (Waterbirds, TravelingBirds, CUB-200) benchmarks, AIM delivers dual benefits: better interpretability, measured by the Energy Pointing Game, and higher accuracy.

How AIM works

AIM augments a standard backbone with a top-down path. At selected encoding stages, a lightweight mask estimator predicts a sample-specific binary mask that selects which spatial features may contribute to the decision. During training, an annealed active-area objective gradually shrinks the allowed mask area, so the network learns to keep only the features that genuinely support the prediction — supervised by nothing but the image label.

The AIM architecture: mask estimators at multiple encoding stages produce binary masks that gate the top-down feature maps used for classification.

Results

Across general-purpose (ImageNet-100, Hard ImageNet, ImageWoof) and fine-grained (Waterbirds, TravelingBirds, CUB-200) benchmarks, AIM improves both classification accuracy and attribution localization (EPG score) over strong baselines — consistently, across backbones.

Try AIM in three lines

AIM scales to full ImageNet-1k. Beyond the benchmarks reported in the paper, we trained AIM on the full ImageNet-1k dataset (ConvNeXt-T, 200 epochs) and release three pretrained models — ready to use via PyTorch Hub: 

import torch

model = torch.hub.load("eyad-al-shami/AIM", "aim_imagenet_anneal_all_alpha10")
masks = model.predict_masks(images)   # one binary mask per FPN level

Checkpoints for CUB-200, TravelingBirds and Waterbirds (25% and 35% active-area variants) are available on the releases page, with a download script and inference example in the repository.
